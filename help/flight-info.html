Flights

Where flight information comes from

Short version: flight times come from airlines and airports, and changes usually show within a few minutes. They can sometimes be late or wrong, so always check with your airline before acting on them.

Where it comes from

Airlines and airports publish their flight times and changes. We get them through our flight information provider, which only ever learns flight numbers and dates, never names.

How up to date it is

Changes usually show on the board within a few minutes. All times are the local time at each airport.

When it's not quite right

The information can sometimes be late, missing or wrong. For example, a flight can show as "Waiting" because there's no status for it yet, or a gate can say "Gate not posted yet". Always check with your airline before acting on it.

Adding flights

  • Type the flight number as it's printed on your ticket and the date it leaves (the local date at the airport you leave from).
  • Airlines publish flights about 11 months ahead, so a flight booked further out can't be added yet.
  • Got a connection? Add each flight on its own. They're put in order for you.

What the board's words mean

  • WaitingThere's no status for this flight yet.
  • On timeIt hasn't left yet, and no delay has been reported.
  • DelayedThe airline has reported a delay. The new time shows next to the old one.
  • In the airIt has taken off. The board shows about how long until it lands.
  • LandedIt's on the ground at the airport.
  • ArrivedAlso on the ground. You may see Landed or Arrived; both mean the flight is down.
  • CancelledThe flight won't go. Once the airline rebooks you, tap "Add my new flight" so everyone sees when you'll really get there.
  • DivertedIt's going to, or has landed at, a different airport. The board says which one.

Terminals, gates and the baggage belt show up as soon as the airport posts them.

A "✓ made it" next to someone's name means they tapped "I made it" themselves. It's never automatic, and it's never a location.
